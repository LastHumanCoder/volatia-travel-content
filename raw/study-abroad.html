Why faculty led programs are harder than they look

A faculty lead is chosen for expertise in art history or public health, not for the ability to manage a coach company in a country where they do not speak the language. Then the program starts and they are responsible for:

  • Twenty or thirty young adults, some travelling internationally for the first time.
  • A duty of care the institution carries whether or not anyone planned for it.
  • Consent forms, medical disclosures and emergency contacts, held under FERPA.
  • Vendors, transport and accommodation negotiated at a distance.
  • Whatever happens at two in the morning, in a language nobody in the group speaks.

That last one is the reason we built this. Everything else a competent travel agency can handle.

Why FERPA matters here

Study abroad programs collect exactly the records FERPA protects: enrolment information, disability accommodations, disciplinary matters, emergency contacts. The moment those documents pass to a translator, the vendor's compliance posture becomes the institution's problem.

Volatia has worked under FERPA with school districts and universities since long before the travel service existed, and we are ISO 27001 certified for information security. That is not a differentiator in the education market, it is a requirement, and plenty of travel providers cannot meet it.

Common questions

What is a faculty led study abroad program?

A short-term program abroad designed and led by a faculty member from the student's own institution, usually running from one to six weeks and carrying academic credit. The faculty lead teaches and also carries responsibility for the group's welfare, which is where most of the administrative burden comes from.

Do you handle the whole program or just the travel?

Travel planning and booking, plus the language layer: interpreters available 24 hours a day in 300+ languages and document translation for consent forms, handbooks and medical paperwork. Academic content stays with the institution.

Are you FERPA compliant?

Yes, and HIPAA compliant, and ISO 27001 and ISO 9001 certified. We have worked under FERPA with school districts and universities since well before our travel service existed.

How does procurement work for a university?

We hold a national cooperative contract covering all 50 states and U.S. territories, which for many institutions removes the need for a separate competitive process. Your procurement office will know whether that applies to you.

What happens if a student needs a doctor and nobody speaks the language?

The faculty lead or the student connects to a professional interpreter by phone or video within seconds, in more than 300 languages, at any hour. That interpreter is provided by the same company that booked the program.
