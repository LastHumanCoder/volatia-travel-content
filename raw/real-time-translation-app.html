How it works in practice

  1. Set it up before you travel. Your account is created and you receive an access code. Install the app from the App Store or Google Play and sign in.
  2. When you need an interpreter, open the app and select the language.
  3. Choose phone or video. Phone is faster and works on weak connections. Video matters when the interpreter needs to see something, or for sign language.
  4. An interpreter joins, normally within seconds.
  5. Put the phone on speaker and let the two of you talk. Speak to the person, not to the interpreter.

There is no booking, no scheduling and no waiting for a callback. This is the difference between having language support and having a phone number for language support.

On interpreter AI

Machine interpretation is improving quickly and it will keep improving. It is already useful for low-stakes conversation, and pretending otherwise would be silly.

What it does not yet do is take responsibility. In a consent conversation, a police interview or a contract negotiation, someone has to be accountable for the accuracy of what was conveyed, able to say "I am not certain, let me check", and bound by a confidentiality obligation. That is a professional standard, not a model capability, and it is why regulated settings still require a human.

Our position is straightforward: use the machine where it is sufficient, and a professional where it is not. The app exists to make reaching the professional as fast as reaching the machine.

Common questions

What is an interpreting app?

An app that connects you to a live professional interpreter rather than machine-translating your words. You select a language and an interpreter joins by phone or video, usually within seconds, in more than 300 languages, at any hour.

How fast does an interpreter join?

Usually within seconds for phone interpreting. Video is similar. There is no booking or scheduling for on-demand use, which is the point of it.

Does it work for sign language?

Yes, by video. Sign language interpreting requires the visual channel, so it is a video service rather than a phone one.

Can interpreter AI replace a human interpreter?

Not in settings where accuracy carries legal or medical consequences. Machine interpretation is useful for low-stakes conversation, but it cannot take responsibility for accuracy, flag its own uncertainty, or be bound by confidentiality obligations.
