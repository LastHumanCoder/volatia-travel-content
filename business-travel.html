What corporate travel usually gets wrong

Corporate travel agents are good at cost control and itineraries. What no corporate travel agent otherwise offers is an interpreter network, and on an international trip that is the gap.

Most business travel programs are built around cost control and duty of care, both of which assume everyone can communicate. On a domestic trip that holds. On an international one it quietly stops being true, and the failure shows up in places nobody budgeted for.

  • A negotiation where your counterpart's English is fine socially and not fine for indemnity clauses.
  • A supplier audit where the plant manager speaks no English and the only interpreter available is the supplier's own employee.
  • A traveller in a hospital, a police station or a customs office at two in the morning.
  • A signed contract that means something slightly different in each language.

None of these are travel problems, which is exactly why a travel agency does not solve them.

How an engagement runs

  1. Scope. Destinations, dates, travellers, meeting schedule and the languages involved.
  2. Itinerary. A plan built around the meetings, with real options and prices.
  3. Language plan. Which sessions need a booked interpreter, which mode, and on-demand access for everyone travelling.
  4. Preparation. Agendas, decks, glossaries and contracts go to the interpreters in advance, and anything that will be relied on gets translated.
  5. Travel. Booked and confirmed, with interpreter access live on every traveller's phone before departure.
  6. During the trip. Interpreting available around the clock. Travel support reachable 8am to 5pm Eastern.

Common questions

Are you a corporate travel agency?

We are a travel planning and booking service run by a language company. We do what a corporate travel agency does, plan and book flights, hotels, cars and ground transport, and we add the piece an agency cannot: professional interpreters in more than 300 languages, available around the clock, from the same organization.

Can you supply an interpreter in the country we are visiting?

Phone, video and virtual platform interpreting work anywhere in the world, in 300+ languages, 24 hours a day. In-person interpreting is provided directly in the United States in 280+ languages. For an in-person interpreter abroad we arrange it as part of the trip plan.

How do we pay for language services?

Three options. An annual plan with a committed budget, which carries the lowest rates. Pay as you go, billed by use. Or a hybrid that begins on annual pricing and switches to pay as you go once the budget is used.

Do you work with public institutions?

Yes. We hold a national cooperative contract covering all 50 states and U.S. territories, which simplifies procurement, and we are ISO 27001 and ISO 9001 certified with HIPAA and FERPA compliance.

How much notice do you need?

On-demand phone and video interpreting needs none. Scheduled onsite or simultaneous interpreting should be booked weeks ahead, because qualified interpreters in a given language pair and subject are limited. Travel booking depends on the route and season.
